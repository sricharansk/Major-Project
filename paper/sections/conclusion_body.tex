We presented a complete, reproducible prototype that joins multi-horizon graph-Transformer forecasting, conformal
calibration, model-faithful explanations, allow-listed retrieval, a locally hosted language model constrained by
deterministic checks, and matched-seed simulation into one auditable advisory workflow on public New York City data,
and we ran it end to end on nearly ten months of real DOT speeds. The measured outcome is mixed and reported as such: the
proposed forecaster beats persistence by 7--16\,\% MAE and the tree baselines at every horizon, and over three seeds
it is 0.03--0.08\,mph below the temporal Transformer at every horizon and the GRU at 30 and 60 minutes, with the GRU
better at 15 minutes, a small advantage rather than a decisive one; the availability gate helps and the operational weather
features hurt; conformal intervals hold their marginal coverage but under-cover congestion; retrieval removes
fabricated citations and the constraint layer, not the model, decides admissibility; no advisory reached the
simulation gate because the model chose monitoring on the mapped corridor; the first controller comparison is
superseded, because an audit found that SUMO had diverted half of the corridor traffic, and the corrected comparison
could only run after two preregistered calibrations, the second of which (a common cycle with 25\,mph progression
offsets) brought the modelled link within 0.9\,mph of its observed speed; on that calibrated scenario testbed the
deterministic forecast-informed rule lowers time loss by about 5\,\% under configured stress while completing more
trips in every seed, a simulated result that is not a real-world gain. Future work, in the order the evidence suggests,
is training with more seeds and without the compute budget, congestion-conditional calibration, a denser sensor network
on which the spatial layer can matter, a testbed with side-street demand, NYC DOT timing plans and pedestrians, the live
observational run, and an externally reviewed retrieval benchmark.
