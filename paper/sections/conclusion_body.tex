We presented a complete, reproducible prototype that joins multi-horizon graph-Transformer forecasting, conformal
calibration, model-faithful explanations, allow-listed retrieval, a locally hosted language model constrained by
deterministic checks, and matched-seed simulation into one auditable advisory workflow on public New York City data,
and we ran it end to end on nine months of real DOT speeds. The measured outcome is mixed and reported as such: the
proposed forecaster beats persistence by 7--16\,\% MAE and the tree baselines at every horizon, but not the GRU or
temporal Transformer by a margin a single seed can establish; the availability gate helps and the operational weather
features hurt; conformal intervals hold their marginal coverage but under-cover congestion; retrieval removes
fabricated citations and the constraint layer, not the model, decides admissibility; no advisory reached the
simulation gate because the model chose monitoring on the mapped corridor; and the simulation testbed's controller
comparison is inconclusive because its declared demand gridlocks the corridor under every controller. Future work, in the order the evidence
suggests, is multi-seed training without the compute budget, congestion-conditional calibration, a denser sensor
network on which the spatial layer can matter, calibrated simulation demand from continuous counts, pedestrian
modelling, and an externally reviewed retrieval benchmark.
