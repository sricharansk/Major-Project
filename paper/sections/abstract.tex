\begin{abstract}
Urban traffic management needs forecasts that are accurate at several horizons, honest about their uncertainty, and
explainable enough to justify an intervention. This paper presents an end-to-end research prototype for Manhattan
that couples a context-gated spatiotemporal graph transformer for 15/30/60-minute link-speed forecasting with a
constrained, retrieval-augmented agentic workflow that produces simulation-only signal-timing advisories. The
forecaster consumes a directed road-link graph built from NYC DOT real-time speed links as a PyTorch Geometric
Temporal signal, fuses weather, 311 and calendar context through an availability-aware gate, and is calibrated
with split conformal prediction. Explanations are computed on the trained model with grouped KernelSHAP. Advisories
are drafted by a locally hosted large language model that must cite passages retrieved from an allowlisted corpus
of federal signal-timing guidance and a project-defined policy, are checked by deterministic safety constraints,
and are evaluated against a baseline controller in SUMO with matched seeds before being queued for human review.
All data are public and all reported figures are generated from stored artifacts of one end-to-end run on nine
months of 2026 NYC DOT speeds (16 usable links). On the 45-day test partition the proposed model reaches an MAE of
4.77, 5.33 and 6.16\,mph at 15, 30 and 60 minutes, 7--16\,\% below persistence and below both tree baselines, but
within 0.05\,mph of a GRU and a temporal-only Transformer; the availability gate improves MAE by 0.07\,mph (bootstrap
interval excluding zero) while the operational weather features worsen it by 0.05\,mph, a negative result. Conformal
intervals reach 0.90--0.91 coverage overall but 0.76--0.80 under congestion, which the deterministic gate converts
into a 26\,\% deferral rate. Retrieval removes fabricated citations (3 of 3 without it, 0 of 9 with it), and the
constraint layer, not the model, decides admissibility; no advisory reached the simulation gate because the model
chose monitoring on the mapped corridor, and the 45-run SUMO controller comparison is inconclusive because the declared
demand gridlocks the corridor under every controller. Negative, partial and not-run outcomes are reported in Section~\ref{sec:results}
exactly as produced by the pipeline.
\end{abstract}
