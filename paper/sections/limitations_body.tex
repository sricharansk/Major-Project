\label{sec:limitations}
The DOT link set is sparse and highway-heavy, so graph neighbours are often distant and the spatial layer has less
to exploit than on a dense sensor network; the full-year link enumeration timed out server-side and the study uses
the 26 Manhattan links active since 2026-09-20; about half of their raw records are zero-speed
no-measurement rows, treated as missing. Monitored segments do not tile the highways, so the graph needs an explicit
corridor-continuity rule and remains sparse. Operational weather is Open-Meteo's historical-forecast product, a re-forecast
proxy rather than an archived agency forecast. Collisions lag publication by months and are retrospective only.
A single seed and a single chronological split are used; the only variance estimate is a day-block bootstrap on
paired differences. Split conformal intervals carry a marginal guarantee under exchangeability, which traffic series
violate. The approved corpus is small and the retrieval labels were drafted by the project without external review.
The language model is a 7B quantised model served locally; its advisories are only as good as the evidence and the
constraints that follow it. The SUMO testbed is a scenario built from OSM geometry with partly assumed demand and
default signal programs, not a calibrated digital twin, and contains no pedestrian demand. Container deployment was
not exercised end to end because of registry rate limits in the development environment. The complete, maintained
list is in \texttt{reports/limitations.md}.
